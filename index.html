About Me

I’m a career changer transitioning into software engineering after seven years working in addiction counseling and public health, where I learned that meaningful technology starts with understanding people and the problems they actually face. I’ve always been drawn to work that helps people, and software has opened a new way for me to do that—by making useful technology more accessible, particularly in the rural community where I now live.

I’m focused on building practical, community-centered applications that solve real problems rather than technology for its own sake. One project I’m developing explores how software can strengthen neighbor-to-neighbor connections by allowing people to exchange resources, skills, and assistance based on actual community needs. My goal is to use software to make it easier for people to find help, offer what they can, and build stronger local networks.

Technically, I’m building full-stack applications with a focus on clean APIs, relational databases, data pipelines, and LLM integration. I’m currently developing a semantic search engine using embeddings and ChromaDB, exploring how vector databases and modern AI techniques can make information more accessible and useful.

For me, software engineering is a continuation of the work I’ve already been doing: helping people solve problems, removing barriers to access, and building practical systems that make communities stronger.
